# Watch it and judge it

## Objective

Watch it, and decide whether it earns its place.

## Acceptance criteria

- The revenue film rendered and watched, not sampled.
- The judgement made explicitly: does the chart answering a sentence read as the same language the
  rest of the deck speaks, or as a highlight pasted onto a photograph?
- If it reads as pasted on, the round says so and the work is discarded rather than defended.
- The sprint closed against its criteria, including the negative ones.

## Judged: it speaks the deck's language, and it earns its place

Watched, not sampled. The verdict the task asked for, plainly: **it reads as the same language the
rest of the deck speaks, not as a highlight pasted onto a photograph.** Four reasons, and the fourth
is the one that decides it:

- The dim is the deck's own ink, so a recessed panel is recessed the way an unreached bullet or an
  unvisited plate already is.
- The mark is the accent at hairline weight, quiet enough at rest to sit beside the axis labels
  rather than on top of them.
- The timing is `anchorState` with no parameters of its own, so the rise, the hold and the settle
  are the ones the ear is already trained on from the first three slides.
- **The rectangle is the shape of the thing, not a shape imposed on it.** A small-multiples panel
  really is a rectangle, so framing one frames the subject exactly.

The last point is also the limit, and it is the honest residue of the round. On a chart whose parts
are *not* rectangular — a scatter cloud, a region of a map, a single line among five — the same
treatment would frame a box of mostly empty space around the thing being talked about. It would
still be correct and it would look like an annotation. Nothing was found to convict it here because
the demo's chart is made of rectangles; whether it generalizes is a question for a chart that is not.

The second thing worth watching for was clutter at the end of the slide, where both reached regions
carry a mark at once. At `markAlpha` 0.5 they read as two quiet frames rather than as a diagram
about itself, and the accumulation is what every other archetype already does. Not changed.

Not discarded. The film is 1:30.8, and the beat at 63 seconds — "Asia Pacific doubles across the
year", and the chart answers — is the thing the round existed to find out was possible.
