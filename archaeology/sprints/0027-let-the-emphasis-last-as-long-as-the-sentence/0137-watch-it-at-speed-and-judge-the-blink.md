# Watch it at speed and judge the blink

## Objective

Watch it and say whether the blink is gone.

## Acceptance criteria

- Judged on the rendered film at full speed, not on a contact sheet — the defect was a perceptual
  one and a still cannot show it.
- The handover between "Asia Pacific doubles" and "Latin America does not move" checked
  specifically, since it is the only one the deck has.
- Depth of the veil re-judged now that it persists: what is bearable for 0.8s may not be for three
  seconds.
- If it still blinks, that is recorded and the round says so.

## The blink is gone, and the release is a settle rather than a snap

The arc, on the film: clean → veil rises over 0.4s → holds for the whole of "Asia Pacific doubles
across the year" → the opening slides right without the veil ever dropping → holds for the whole of
"Latin America does not move" → settles back over 0.9s, finishing into the slide's own transition.

Both halves of the original complaint are answered. The dim no longer arrives and leaves inside a
second, because it now lasts as long as the sentence that caused it — about three seconds each here
rather than 0.8. And the return is a settle: at frame 2046 still veiled, 2054 partway, 2062 fully
back, which is 0.9 seconds rather than half of one.

**The depth was re-judged now that it persists and it needed to come down.** 0.66 was chosen for a
flash; held for three seconds it reads as switching the chart off rather than as recessing it.
At 0.55 the dimmed bars stay plainly legible — `anchor.ts` is explicit that a recessive element must
remain readable — while the lit panel is unambiguous.

One thing looked at and left alone: mid-handover the opening is a window spanning half of Asia
Pacific and half of Latin America, because it is interpolating between two adjacent rectangles. It
reads as a spotlight sliding, which is what it is. A cut would have been simpler and would have been
a second flash.

Not recorded as a residual defect. The round's complaint is answered.
