# Watch it at every scale, and fix what watching finds

## Objective

Watch it, and fix what watching finds.

This round has three things nobody has seen: a formula at the bottom of two chambers, two sibling
scopes entered one after the other, and five excursions in a piece that is only twice as long as
the one that had two. Each of those is a perceptual question and none of them can be answered by a
test.

Specific things to watch for, because they are predictions rather than hopes:

- **Two rooms in a row.** dragon:9 predicts this either makes the middle world feel like
  connective tissue worth keeping, or makes it feel like a corridor between destinations. The
  moment that decides it is the return from `schedule` — whether seeing it back in its place, with
  `round` still to the right of it, is worth the four seconds it costs.
- **Stacked vignettes.** Every atlas paints its own edge darkening. Three of them stacked may read
  as depth or as mud.
- **The formula at depth.** Three chambers deep, mathematics has to be legible and has to look
  *set* rather than pasted.
- **Threshold budget.** Five excursions at about three and a half seconds each is a seventh of the
  runtime spent travelling, which is the first real pressure on dragon:12.

## Acceptance criteria

- Frames extracted and inspected at: root context, entry to the compression, entry to the
  schedule, the sigma formula, return to the compression, entry to the round, both round formulas,
  return to the compression, and return to root.
- Anything that reads as wrong is fixed in the artifact first and in the machinery only if the
  artifact cannot be fixed.
- The judgement about two sibling rooms is written down whichever way it comes out, and dragon:9
  is updated with it.
- The three existing showpieces still render identically.
